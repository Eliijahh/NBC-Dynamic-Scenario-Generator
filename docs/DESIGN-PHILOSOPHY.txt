NBC DYNAMIC SCENARIO GENERATOR
Campaign Command Functional Design Document
A single-player campaign layer for NEBULOUS: Fleet Command


OVERVIEW
========

Campaign Command turns unrelated NEBULOUS skirmishes into one continuous
squadron command loop. The player keeps one named fleet and one shared pool of
equipment, refits the fleet in the game's native editor, generates a random
opponent, fights a normal local skirmish, and receives an automatic debrief.

The app is the command post. NEBULOUS is the workshop and battlefield. The
mod's job is continuity: the fleet maintained between battles is the fleet
taken into the next one.

    A persistent squadron-management wrapper that lets the player refit a
    shared fleet in the native editor, generate a plausible opponent, fight
    a real skirmish, and keep the aftermath.

The player installs the NBC Campaign Bridge once, enables it in Mods, and then
works mainly from Campaign Command. This describes the mod as built; future
ideas are marked and are not commitments.


DESIGN PILLARS
==============

  - Continuity: ships keep names, identities, loadouts and history. The fleet
    is the main character, so changes are explicit and reversible until
    committed.
  - Native fidelity: refits use the game's native editor, and generated
    enemies are fitted from rules and stock data exported by the game. The app
    manages ownership and campaign state without inventing part statistics.
  - Parts, not prices: equipment is physical and shared. A part removed from
    one ship can go onto another. There is no money or shipyard.
  - Safe commitment: a refit is a draft until confirmed. The player can review,
    continue editing or cancel and keep the approved fleet.
  - Player command, game combat: the mod prepares the context; the player
    deploys, manoeuvres and fights.
  - Visible state: the player knows the phase, what is pending and what to do
    next. Limits are stated plainly and errors name the recovery action.


PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================

The player commands a small squadron, not an empire or factory. They decide
which ship gets the last working sensor, who signs off on a risky refit, and
who reads the report afterwards. The fantasy is competence under constraint: a
small, familiar force where every ship has a name and every part a history.

Goals: ownership, continuity, safe experimentation, native fidelity, and a flow
that needs no file management. The player should also be told what the
prototype does and does not model.

Non-goals: a 4X campaign, economic simulation, autobattler, multiplayer
campaign or scenario editor. It is not a replacement for the native editor.


CORE LOOP
=========

    Fleet roster -> Inventory -> Native refit -> Enemy generation
        -> Live battle -> Debrief -> back to fleet

The refit micro-loop is: remove a part, see it become available, install it on
another ship, review the changes, then confirm or continue editing. The shared
pool turns fitting into a squadron-level decision.

The meta loop is: maintain the fleet, choose opposition, fight, read the
debrief, adjust for the next engagement. It currently has no rewards, losses,
repairs or resource pressure.


FLEET ROSTER
============

ACTIONS: Load a template, import or export a .fleet, create a disposable
three-ship test fleet, rename ships, edit hull numbers, inspect equipment and
start a refit. The app has five tabs: Fleet, Inventory, Refit, Battle, Debrief.

RULES
  - 1 to 10 ships; unique, non-empty names; unique identities and valid hulls.
  - Names must be unique because native reports identify ships by name.
  - Imported fleets are validated before becoming the campaign fleet.
  - Hull, faction and ship identity cannot change during a refit, and roster
    changes are locked.
  - Replacing the roster with the test fleet backs up the previous one.

INTENT: The fleet is the campaign's main character. Names and hull numbers are
the player's anchors, so they are editable outside the editor, while fitting
stays native. The app offers only identity editing to avoid teaching two
versions of the rules. The test fleet is an onboarding sandbox, not an
acquisition path.

LIMITS: No ship acquisition, crew management, veterancy or condition. Hull
changes require choosing another design. Imported designs may carry cached
points until the game recalculates them.


INVENTORY
=========

ACTIONS: Browse the game-exported catalogue, see installed and spare counts,
acquire spares, release spares, check ownership before a refit.

RULES
  - Store stock is unlimited; there is no currency, price, installation cost
    or shipyard requirement.
  - Acquiring adds spares; releasing removes spares only. Installed components
    must be refitted out and confirmed before they can be released.
  - Quantity per transaction is 1 to 999.
  - Acquiring requires the component to be in the game's catalogue.
  - Inventory is locked while a refit or battle is pending.
  - Purchases made before a refit survive cancellation.

SUPPLY MODEL: total owned = installed on ships + spare units in the warehouse.
A refit may use installed parts and spares, but never more of a component than
the fleet owns. Removing a part makes it available elsewhere; installing
consumes a spare; leaving a part removed turns it into a spare on confirmation.

INTENT: The inventory makes equipment feel physical and shared, creating
cross-ship decisions without currency. Money is deferred until the refit and
ownership pipeline is proven.

CURRENT TENSION: Because stock is unlimited and free, this is a bookkeeping
constraint, not an economy. The real constraint is fitting: sockets, power,
crew, magazines and warnings. Refits get those rules from the native editor;
generated enemies get them from the bridge-exported catalogue and the offline
fitter. Keeping the two paths in agreement is the main fitting risk.


NATIVE REFIT
============

FLOW
  1. Start a refit from Fleet or Refit.
  2. The game launches and opens the whole fleet in the normal editor.
  3. Remove, install and rearrange components across ships.
  4. Use the in-game overlay to review the refit.
  5. Inspect the changes in Campaign Command.
  6. Confirm, continue editing, or cancel.

IN-GAME CAMPAIGN RULES
  - The palette shows only usable equipment and current spare counts.
  - Removing a part makes it available to the rest of the fleet immediately.
  - Installing consumes a spare or a part freed elsewhere.
  - Native socket, power, crew, magazine and warning rules still apply.
  - Undo/redo is blocked if it would duplicate or over-allocate a part.
  - Whole-fitting paste, roster changes and fleet load/save are disabled.
  - Leaving the editor is intercepted so the player can review or discard.

DRAFT, REVIEW, COMMIT
    Confirm refit      Validate, reconcile inventory, commit fleet and spares.
    Continue editing   Open a new revision from the draft or last input; the
                       approved fleet remains unchanged.
    Cancel refit       Discard the session; approved fleet and pre-refit
                       spares remain.

Review lists equipment changes by ship and socket, plus identity changes, and
shows the spares that will remain. Confirmation is only available from review.
Resuming after review opens the exported draft; resuming before review opens
the last input. Unsaved editor changes are lost on a crash.

INTENT: The native editor is the best fitting interface and the one the player
already knows. Wrapping it keeps the game's rules authoritative. The shared
pool turns single-ship editing into a squadron puzzle: stripping a part changes
what every other ship can mount.

LIMITS: Refits require launching the game and waiting. Only one refit can be
active, and only one app window may edit a save directory. There are no refit
costs, times, facilities or engineering risks. Confirmation is deliberately an
external action.


ENEMY GENERATION
================

FLOW: Choose Protectorate or Alliance, set a target from 1 to 30,000 points
(default 700), generate, inspect the roster, and reroll before launch if
desired. Generation is local and synchronous: it does not open the fleet editor
or launch the game. A completed generation replaces the previous enemy.

CATALOGUE: On startup the bridge exports Format 1 stock fitting data to
Saves/NBCBridge/fitting-catalog.xml. It contains faction hulls and sockets;
component sizes, socket types, overhang and rotation rules, tiling, point and
compounding costs, crew, resources and role flags; bulk-magazine capacity and
accepted ammunition; weapon compatibility; and munition volume and point data.
The bridge remains the source of the numbers. Python does not guess part stats.

RULES
  - Stock hulls, components and munitions for the chosen faction only.
  - The fitter installs a drive, powerplant, command module, sensor, at least
    one offensive direct-fire weapon, and a bulk magazine when ammunition is
    needed. It fills most remaining sockets with plausible support systems,
    weapons or defences.
  - Socket type, dimensions, overhang and rotation must pass the native fit
    test. Tiled compartments multiply volume, cost and per-unit resources.
  - Power must balance. Crew uses the hull complement as a soft limit. Ammo is
    limited by magazine volume and point cost.
  - Missile launchers, hangars, craft and missile ammunition are excluded in
    this version.
  - Up to 24 candidate ships are produced, with a hard limit of 192 attempts.
  - The app selects up to 10 ships closest to the target budget and renames
    them "Hostile 01", "Hostile 02", and so on.
  - The actual total may differ from the target. The target is an aim, not a
    guarantee.

INTENT: Random generation gives replayability without hand-authored lists.
Using the game's exported fitting data keeps the opponent plausible under the
same rules the player uses, while avoiding the slow and fragile editor-launch
loop. The budget is the main difficulty dial; faction changes the available
pool. The generator does not counter the player's fleet.

LIMITS: No difficulty presets, doctrines or enemy personalities. No relationship
between enemy budget and player fleet points. No rewards or campaign
consequences. One AI fleet on the default map. Stock content only. v1 has no
missiles, hangars, craft, refit-time or ammunition inventory tracking. Native
revalidation still happens when the battle handoff loads the fleets; a generated
design that the game rejects must be regenerated.


BATTLE AND DEBRIEF
==================

FLOW
  1. Confirm fleet and enemy.
  2. Click "Run battle -> NEBULOUS".
  3. The game launches, or an existing game at the main menu is reused.
  4. The mod creates a local single-player skirmish, assigns both fleets,
     validates the settings, and launches.
  5. The player deploys and fights normally.
  6. When the battle ends, the report is imported automatically and the app
     brings the debrief forward.

GUARANTEES
  - A normal local skirmish, not a custom combat simulation.
  - Player fleet versus one AI enemy, with deployment and tactics under player
    control.
  - The game's default map and scenario.
  - An existing editor or battle is left alone; the mod waits for the main menu.
  - Incompatible fleets, missing mods and missing components are reported before
    launch.
  - If the game does not retain the fleet assignments, the mod reports an error.
  - A failed save or import leaves the game open.
  - Cancelling tracking never terminates a live game.
  - Shutdown follows a safely received result, or an idle game the campaign
    launched and the player cancelled. No live game is force-killed.

DEBRIEF: Shows outcome, duration, per-ship side, status, condition, crew and
damage dealt. History persists by unique battle ID. Raw reports and input fleets
are archived. Duplicate imports are prevented; unknown or duplicate player ships
are rejected. Cancelled battles produce no result.

INTENT: The handoff removes file management from the routine. The mod prepares
a validated match and then lets the player play the game. The debrief provides
closure and records the data needed for future consequences.

LIMITS: One map, one AI opponent, no objectives or scripted events, no
multiplayer, one pending battle at a time. The game must be at the main menu.
Startup can take minutes. A battle that closes without a report cannot produce
a debrief.

Damage and casualties are recorded but not applied to future battles. There are
no repairs, permanent losses, rewards, reputation or experience. The debrief
does not yet change campaign state beyond history and stored ship results.


PROGRESSION AND PERSISTENCE
===========================

Progress is horizontal and logistical: fleet composition, ship names and
identities, shared spares, refit record, battle history and stored per-ship
results. There is no level, currency, technology tree or reputation.

The approved fleet and inventory survive restarts. Pending refits and battles
are remembered and resumable; enemy generation commits immediately. Purchases
made before a session survive cancellation. The approved fleet changes only when
a refit is confirmed. One app window edits a given save directory at a time.

CURRENT GAP: There is no mechanical reason to win or lose beyond the player's
goals. Battles do not consume resources, grant rewards, damage the fleet or
change the enemy pool. The campaign is a continuity scaffold, not yet a complete
game loop with stakes.


UX AND FEEDBACK
===============

The app is a command console: dark background, teal accent, numbered tabs,
uppercase labels and terse operational copy. The player is treated as a
commander who wants the next action.

Feedback is layered: a status line for the current phase or error; an in-game
overlay for inventory, review/cancel and blocked actions; confirmations for
destructive actions; and automatic focus plus a bell when a draft or debrief is
ready. Conflicting actions are disabled, errors name the recovery step, and the
player is never silently moved to another fleet, battle or revision.

The main friction is bridge setup, game launch time, knowing that confirmation
happens outside the game, and watching two applications. Status, resume and
reuse of an already-open game mitigate this but do not remove it.


BALANCE AND CONTENT
===================

TUNING SNAPSHOT
    Fleet size                     1 to 10 ships
    Enemy target budget            1 to 30,000 points; default 700
    Buy/sell quantity              1 to 999 units
    Enemy candidate pool           up to 24 valid designs, 192 attempts
    Enemy selection                up to 10 ships, closest to budget
    Hull eligibility               stock hulls at or below max(200, budget)
    Refit revisions                unlimited through resume, one at a time
    Store stock / currency         unlimited / none
    Refit cost / repairs / losses  none

Difficulty comes from enemy faction, target points, the player's fleet
composition and how much inventory they commit to each ship. There is no hidden
scaling; the enemy is not matched to the player's points and does not counter
their loadout.

Scarcity currently comes from owned part quantities, socket compatibility, hull
limits, power and crew budgets, opportunity cost when moving a part, and
patience. It does not come from money, storage, maintenance, refit time or loss.
Free purchasing makes the inventory a ledger rather than an economy.

CONTENT: Two stock factions, installed and imported fleets, stock components,
the three-ship Raines test fleet, one default skirmish map, generated enemies and
an open-ended chain of battles. No authored missions, objectives, narrative,
territory, calendar or progression content. No multiplayer, tactical automation,
map selection, repairs, damage carryover or economy.


OPEN QUESTIONS
==============

  - Where does scarcity come from? Free stock removes economic pressure.
    Directions: requisition points, limited stock, salvage, maintenance or
    refit time.
  - Why fight? Battles grant no resources, reputation or permanent change.
    Directions: salvage, currency, ship experience, faction standing or
    survival pressure.
  - Should damage persist? The debrief records condition, crew and damage, but
    the next battle ignores it. Applying it and adding repairs would give
    battles weight.
  - Is the native editor too heavy for refits? Launching the game for every
    refit is slow. Options: batch refits, a planning view that does not replace
    native validation, or quick swaps for owned parts. Enemy generation no
    longer launches the game.
  - Does the offline fitter drift from the native rules? The bridge exports
    fitting data, but Python reproduces selection, resources, tiling and costs.
    Options: add a bridge-side validation pass, compare generated fits against
    the editor, or keep a curated regression catalogue from a known game build.
  - How should difficulty be expressed? Enemy budget is self-selected and
    unrelated to player strength. Consider suggested budgets, presets,
    doctrines or missions with fixed opposition.
  - Does "buy/sell" mislead? The words imply an economy that does not exist.
    Rename to "Requisition" and "Release", or add a real cost.


FUTURE DIRECTIONS
=================

NEAR-TERM: Apply damage and casualties to the next battle; add repairs, salvage
or rewards; add requisition pressure; rename buy/sell if no economy is added;
add difficulty presets and a first-run checklist.

MEDIUM-TERM: Ship histories and veterancy, permanent losses and replacement
hulls, limited supply, refit time and maintenance, enemy doctrines, mission
objectives and a campaign calendar.

LONG-TERM: Authored chapters, faction relationships, territory or supply lines,
multi-battle operations, richer after-action narrative, and optional multiplayer
only after single-player is stable.

GUARDRAILS: The game remains the authority on fitting and combat. The player
remains in command. A draft is never committed without confirmation. No approved
fleet or inventory is lost to a failed operation. New systems must justify their
complexity in the core loop.


ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
===================

  - A new player can complete fleet -> inventory -> refit -> battle -> debrief
    without manually editing campaign files.
  - The player can explain why a component is unavailable and distinguish
    Confirm, Continue and Cancel.
  - Cancelling a refit or battle never loses the approved fleet; regenerating
    an enemy never damages the player fleet.
  - The fleet never owns more copies of a component than the player owns, and
    undo cannot duplicate equipment.
  - A refit cannot change faction, hull or ship identities.
  - A battle report cannot be imported twice or silently add unknown ships.
  - The fleet feels persistent, refitting feels native, and the enemy feels like
    a plausible product of the same rules.
  - The debrief arrives automatically and the player always has a visible next
    action or waiting reason.
  - Closing and reopening the app preserves the approved fleet, inventory and
    pending operations; a failed save leaves the previous state intact.
  - No live game is force-closed while it may contain unsaved work.


FINAL STATEMENT
===============

    Give the player one fleet they can keep, one shared toolbox they must
    allocate, and one native editor where the rules still matter. Make the
    refit a draft, make the battle real, and make the aftermath automatic.

The build delivers continuity and the safe refit loop. It does not yet deliver
scarcity, consequence or reward. Those are the next design problems, and the
existing debrief and inventory systems are the foundation for them.
